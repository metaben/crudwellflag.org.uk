Some initial thoughts on how to start and run your own Flood Action Group:

## Start with Why

- why are you starting a group?
- what community will be served by the group? what do they want? what do they need?
- what area will it cover?
- who do you hope will join your group? why?
- what do you want the group to achieve in the short and long term?

## Knowledge

To build confidence and credibility, build your knowledge:

- find out how homes, property, businesses, farmland have been affected by floods (talk to your neighbours and their neighbours?)
- know the land, know the watercourses:
  - where does the water come from? where does it go to?
  - has it always been like this? when did things change? [check local and national maps](/blog/useful-maps)
  - are there any conservation areas or SSSIs?
  - what land is at particular risk of flooding?
- understand the differences between:
  - rivers, streams, ditches, watercourses, flood plains
  - groundwater, surface water, flash flooding, field run-off
  Riparian Responsibility
  - [rules and permissions](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/owning-a-watercourse) (Environment Agency)
  - [rights and roles](https://engageenvironmentagency.uk.engagementhq.com/your-watercourse-rights-and-roles) (Environment Agency)
  - [who is responsible?](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10746/) (Parliament)
- understand infrastructure and what it does:
  - drains, gulleys, culverts, trash screens, sluices, weirs, swales
  - embankments, spoil, berms, bunds, dredgings
  - water pipes, sewage pipes, sewage pumping stations, conduits for other utilities
- understand roles and responsibilities:
  - what is the town or parish council responsible for?
  - what about the local authority? do they have a working group responsible for flood coordination?
  - [Drainage Boards](https://www.ada.org.uk/)
  - the [Environment Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/environment-agency/about)
  - [which water company](https://www.water.org.uk/customers/find-your-supplier) supplies water and processes sewage in your area?
  - [Flood Wardens](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/could-you-be-a-volunteer-flood-warden) - how many do you have? how many do you need?
  - [LRFs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-role-of-local-resilience-forums-a-reference-document) - should offer training
  - how well prepared are residents?
- what opportunities are there? what can be improved, how, and why?

## Members of your group

These sorts of people might be good for your FLAG:

- people who care about the causes of flooding, and are willing to do something about it.
- poeople who are practical and hands-on, who can safely participate in activities in/around watercourses.
- people who get out and about, perhaps they walk their dog along the river?
- nature-lovers, people concerned about wildlife and ecology.
- somebody willing to organise the group (bonus skills: a good listener, communicator, decision-maker, can work with the system not against it).

## Watercourse maintenance

It's extremely likely you'll need to look at maintenance. The aim is not to scour and dig everything out! The aim is manage water responsibly, while minimising unecessary risk.

- where did floods happen last time? was maintenance a factor?
- which watercourses need maintenance? and then:
  - when?
  - what sort of maintenance?
  - how frequently?
  - who should/must do it?
  - is a volunteer working party required?
  - what are the environmental and wildlife considerations?
  - is permission required?
  - what tools or machinery is required?
  - where will silt or vegetation be put after? who will move it? 

## Communication

- how will you meet/talk as a group?
  - chat group
    - single group
    - multiple groups (eg. planning/steering group, or town/parish sub-regions)
  - online forum
  - in person
  - email
- how will you engage with your community?
  - new/existing website
  - global platform (eg. facebook group)
  - town/parish website/newsletter
- what do you want your relationship to be with your Town/Parish Council?
  - owned/controlled by the TC/PC
  - sub-committee to the TC/PC
  - independent but working closely
  - independent but it's complicated (how will you talk to authorities without them?)
